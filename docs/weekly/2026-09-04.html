SHEIN GLOBAL HOLDINGS LIMITED (0625.HK) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Consumer / E-Commerce — Fast Fashion Market Cap: ~$20.8B today (HK$38.14 x 4.23bn shares); $26.4B at the IPO price | Index: HKEX main board, SHEIN-W Closest comps: WMT, LULU, CROX, FIVE M&A FLAG: None. The relevant corporate event is the listing itself: a Hong Kong IPO priced 2026-08-31 after a London listing stalled.

1. Business Description

We sell clothes on the internet, very cheaply, and the interesting part is not the clothes.

We design and source apparel, beauty products and homeware from a network of several thousand contract manufacturers, concentrated in and around Guangzhou, and sell directly to consumers in the United States, Europe, the Middle East and Asia through our own apps and websites. We make money on the spread between what we pay a factory and what a shopper pays us, and increasingly on commission from third-party sellers who list on our marketplace alongside our own goods.

What distinguishes us mechanically is the size of the first production run. A conventional apparel retailer commits to a season months ahead, orders thousands of units per style, and discounts what does not sell — markdowns are the industry's defining cost. We start at a few hundred units, watch what moves in the first days, and reorder only that. The consequence is that we can carry hundreds of thousands of live SKUs and introduce thousands more a day without carrying the inventory risk that volume normally implies. The factory network is the asset: it is organised around very short runs and very fast turnarounds, which is not something a competitor can buy off the shelf.

The second structural feature is fulfilment. We have historically shipped direct from China to the customer under de minimis exemptions that let low-value parcels enter major markets without duty. As those exemptions have narrowed, the model has had to absorb duty and move toward local warehousing — which is the single largest change to our unit economics in the company's history, and the reason a company privately marked at $100bn in 2022 listed at roughly a quarter of that.

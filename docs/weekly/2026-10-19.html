Three refusals that override everything above

  • No market cap means no auto-add, ever. The size buckets are gated on it, and for Bucket 1 — which has no floor — it stands in for “does this actually trade yet”. A Form 10 SpinCo has a core SIC code and no shares, so it cannot auto-add on the sector alone. A cap written as prose (~$3.7B) is refused too, and says so rather than claiming the cap is missing.
  • A ticker on the provenance removals list is never auto-added. A vendor has no idea a row was deliberately removed and will keep re-proposing it. An FMP biopharma screen re-proposed ALBT eight days after it was removed for leaving healthcare.
  • Auto means unasked, not unvalidated. Every auto-add routes through the same enrichment gate as a manual one, which refuses a half-filled row and says why.

City Therapeutics, Inc. (CTY) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Biotech (RNAi therapeutics) Market Cap: ~$1.12B at $21.74 (Est.: ~51.7M post-IPO shares; $0.93B at the $18 offer) | Index: Newly public (Nasdaq Global Market, first trade 2026-10-16) Closest comps: ALNY, ARWR, IONS, WVE M&A FLAG: None

1. Business Description

We make siRNA drugs: short double-stranded RNA molecules that, injected under the skin a few times a year, shut off a single disease-causing gene in a chosen tissue. Alnylam proved that this works and that it can be a very large business. Our co-founder and executive chair, John Maraganore, was Alnylam's founding CEO, and our CEO, Andy Orth, led the launch of the first approved RNAi drug. We were founded in 2023 to run that playbook again with newer chemistry.

Our platform has three parts. First, engineered RNAi "triggers" (cityRNA), which we pick partly with an in-house machine-learning model. Second, delivery conjugates: GalNAc for the liver, and our own LRP1 ligands to reach the eye, brain and fat, tissues GalNAc cannot. Third, target selection driven by human genetics.

Our lead, CITY-FXI, silences Factor XI, a clotting protein. People born with little Factor XI rarely have thrombosis and rarely bleed badly, which is why the whole industry is chasing it as a safer anticoagulant. In healthy volunteers a single dose cut Factor XI by more than 60% at 15 mg, close to 80% at 50 mg, and 85% at three weeks after 150 mg. More Phase 1 data is due in late 2026, and a Phase 2 trial in total knee replacement is planned for mid-2027. We plan to find a partner before Phase 3. CITY-RBP4, for Stargardt disease (and later geographic atrophy), should enter Phase 1 in late 2026. CITY-TFR2, for anemia of chronic disease, starting with myelofibrosis, begins a China investigator-initiated trial in late 2026.

Our revenue is research funding from two partners: Bausch + Lomb (one retinal target, signed July 2024) and Biogen (neurodegeneration, signed May 2025; Biogen's convertible note converts into 2.13M shares at the IPO). Collaboration revenue was $13.6M in 2025. Net loss was $58.5M in 2025 and $53.4M in the first half of 2026, so spending is accelerating.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - Factor XI is one of the few large-market cardiovascular targets left. A twice-yearly subcutaneous shot would compete on convenience with antibody FXI programs (abelacimab, osocimab) and Bayer's oral asundexian. - The management team has done this before. Alnylam's founding team plus ARCH, Fidelity, Viking and Invus is a credible syndicate, and the deal was upsized 14% at the midpoint. - The LRP1 conjugates, if they work in the eye and brain, are a platform asset partners pay for. Biogen and Bausch + Lomb already do.

Bear case: - Factor XI's clinical record is mixed. Bayer's asundexian failed OCEANIC-AF against apixaban in 2023, and the field's Phase 3s are still reading out. An RNAi entrant in Phase 1 is years behind antibody programs and plans to partner before Phase 3, which caps its economics. - The 1H26 burn ($53.4M) is running near twice the 2025 rate. With ~$0.33B of post-IPO cash, a follow-on before Phase 2 data is likely. - RNAi is increasingly crowded (Alnylam, Arrowhead, plus Chinese entrants), so differentiation rests on unproven extrahepatic delivery.

Key swing factor: Whether Factor XI's Phase 3 readouts (antibody and small-molecule) validate the target, which would set the value of a Phase 1 RNAi asset.

Iambic Therapeutics, Inc. (IAM) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Biotech (AI-enabled small-molecule oncology) Market Cap: ~$0.76B at the $16 offer; ~$0.90B at the 10-15 close of $18.91 | Index: Newly public (Nasdaq Global Select, 2026-10-15) Closest comps: RXRX, SDGR, ABSI, ABCL M&A FLAG: None

1. Business Description

We are a drug company that happens to own its own design software, and the software is the reason we exist. Our models predict how a candidate molecule will bind its target and behave in the body before anyone makes it, which lets a small chemistry team cycle through designs faster than a traditional medicinal-chemistry group. We then take the best molecules into the clinic ourselves. The payoff we are selling is not the platform as a product; it is the drugs.

Our lead drug, IAM1363, is an oral, irreversible HER2 kinase inhibitor engineered to hit HER2 while sparing EGFR, a related receptor whose inhibition causes the rash and diarrhoea that cap the dose of older HER2 pills. At ESMO 2025 we reported partial responses in 28% of 18 heavily pretreated patients with measurable disease, many already treated with Enhertu (T-DXd) and tucatinib, and in 1 of 3 patients with measurable brain metastases. The Phase 1/1b trial in HER2-altered solid tumours is ongoing. Behind it, we filed an IND for IAM217, a KIF18A inhibitor aimed at chromosomally unstable tumours, on 2026-09-30, and a CDK2/4 inhibitor (IAM-C1) is expected to reach IND in Q4 2026.

We make some money from partners who use the platform: collaboration revenue was $9.4M in 2025 and $12.8M in the first half of 2026. That does not come close to covering the cost of running trials — we lost $77.3M in 2025 and $50.1M in 1H26. Nvidia is an investor, which is why every headline calls us "Nvidia-backed"; ARK and Duquesne indicated up to $60M of the IPO.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - IAM1363's EGFR-sparing design is a real mechanical advantage. If the dose can rise without EGFR toxicity, responses in post-Enhertu patients (28% PR in a refractory population) could support a third-line HER2 label with CNS activity. - About $0.41B of pro forma cash against a ~$0.76B offer cap means the market is paying roughly $0.35B for the pipeline plus the platform. - Three programs reach the clinic within about a year (HER2 now, KIF18A and CDK2/4 by early 2027). That is the speed claim being tested in public.

Bear case: - HER2 is crowded. Enhertu dominates later lines, and tucatinib, zongertinib (Boehringer) and sevabertinib (Bayer) already compete as selective oral HER2 TKIs. An 18-patient response rate does not establish differentiation. - KIF18A has already burned others: Amgen dropped its program, and the class's clinical validation is thin. - "AI drug discovery" has not re-rated in public markets. RXRX trades near $3.74 after a dilutive $200M raise this week, and SDGR and ABSI are well below their highs. The platform premium is a narrative risk, not a floor.

Key swing factor: Whether IAM1363's expansion cohorts show durable responses after Enhertu at a dose free of EGFR toxicity.

What that ruling was based on. Buckets 1 and 5 are genuinely discretionary, so they were not opened on the “mandatory anyway” reasoning that covers 2 and 3. Three refusals that override everything above

  • No market cap means no auto-add, ever. The size buckets are gated on it, and for Bucket 1 — which has no floor — it stands in for “does this actually trade yet”. A Form 10 SpinCo has a core SIC code and no shares, so it cannot auto-add on the sector alone. A cap written as prose (~$3.7B) is refused too, and says so rather than claiming the cap is missing.
  • A ticker on the provenance removals list is never auto-added. A vendor has no idea a row was deliberately removed and will keep re-proposing it. An FMP biopharma screen re-proposed ALBT eight days after it was removed for leaving healthcare.
  • Auto means unasked, not unvalidated. Every auto-add routes through the same enrichment gate as a manual one, which refuses a half-filled row and says why.

What happens to your reply

A poller reads #ipo-spinoffs-newissues at 09:20, 13:20 and 18:20 ET. It reads both top-level channel messages and thread replies — where you post makes no difference. It only ever acts on a ticker already sitting pending in the ledger, it ignores everyone but you, and it is idempotent by message timestamp so a transient failure cannot re-fire an approval. It answers in the thread with what landed and what did not, then republishes exports/ so the change reaches the downstream consumers.

Company Summaries

Anthropic, PBC (ANT)

We build the Claude family of large AI models and sell access to them, mostly to businesses and software developers. About 79% of our revenue is consumption-based (customers pay per unit of model usage through our platform or the AWS, Google Cloud and Azure marketplaces) and about 21% is subscriptions to our own applications. Revenue was $33.2B for the nine months to 2026-09-30, up from $2.5B a year earlier. Operating loss for the nine months was $1.3B, and compute cost 57% of revenue.

Zoar Limited (ZOAR)

We develop and distribute active pharmaceutical ingredients and intermediates across 35+ products, with some contract development work. We sell direct and through about 100 distributors in 35+ countries outside China, and 56 contract manufacturers make the product for us. Revenue was $651.7M and net income $77.7M in the year to 2026-03-31. We are run from Hong Kong, and our founder holds about 93% of the shares.

Windward Bio Group AG (WWB)

We develop long-acting antibodies for asthma, COPD and atopic dermatitis. Our lead, WIN378, blocks TSLP and is built for dosing twice a year instead of monthly. It is in a Phase 2/3 trial in severe uncontrolled asthma, with Phase 2 results due in 1H 2027. We have no product revenue and lost $42.6M in 1H 2026.

vVARDIS Holding AG (VVVV)

We sell Curodont, a liquid a dentist or hygienist paints onto an early-stage cavity to regrow enamel, so the tooth needs no drilling or filling. Dental practices buy it from us, mostly in the United States. Net revenue for the nine months to 09-30-2026 was $47.7–49.2M (preliminary), against $22.3M a year earlier, with a net loss of about $40M.

Sector / Industry: Tech / AI Models & Platforms Market Cap: ~$1,285B basic at the 2026-11-19 close of $97.17 (13.22B Class A + Class N shares legally outstanding after the offering and private placement, 424B4 dated 2026-11-18); ~$1,762B fully diluted (18.14B shares, 424B4 capitalization table, treasury-stock method at $83) | Index: None yet; listed Nasdaq Global Select 2026-11-19. 1. Business Description

We build the Claude family of large AI models and sell access to them, mostly to businesses and software developers. About 79% of our revenue is consumption-based (customers pay per unit of model usage through our Claude Platform or a cloud marketplace) and about 21% is subscriptions to our own applications such as Claude and Claude Code. Revenue was $33.2B for the nine months to 2026-09-30, up from $2.5B a year earlier, and $17.0B in the third quarter alone.

We were founded in 2021 and started selling in the first quarter of 2023. Revenue was $41M in 2023, $386M in 2024 and $4.6B in 2025. The 2026 step-up came from coding and agent workloads: customers put Claude into software development and other internal workflows and then raised usage quickly. We had more than 11,900 customers with at least $100,000 of trailing-twelve-month revenue at 2026-09-30 (more than 950 a year earlier), more than 1,900 above $1M and about 250 above $10M. Customers above $100,000 produced 74% of nine-month revenue. No customer exceeded 10% of revenue, and customers outside our top five were 79% of third-quarter revenue. Net revenue retention was about 900%. The United States was 71% of nine-month revenue.

We reach customers three ways: direct sales, our own applications, and the AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure marketplaces. Marketplace sales were 44% of nine-month 2026 revenue, so three companies that also compete with us carry a large share of our distribution and our receivables.

Our main cost is compute. Compute and infrastructure expense was $18.8B in the nine months to 2026-09-30, 57% of revenue, against 172% a year earlier. We buy most capacity under multi-year contracts signed 12 to 18 months ahead, and we top up with short-term capacity that costs much more. In the third quarter we took $5.0B of short-term compute, which is why operating income of $974M in the second quarter became an operating loss of $567M in the third. We have committed about $900.9B to purchase and lease agreements, of which $451.6B falls before the end of 2030 and $70.7B in the twelve months after 2026-09-30. We run on four chip families (AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA and AMD GPUs), which lets us move workloads to the cheapest available hardware.

Our reported net loss is dominated by non-cash items. The $104.0B net loss for the nine months includes $94.3B of fair-value changes on convertible notes held mostly by Amazon, plus $10.4B of other expense tied to partner financing arrangements. Operating loss was $1.3B; our adjusted operating income, which excludes stock compensation and similar items, was $4.1B. We had about 5,300 employees at 2026-09-30.

We are a Delaware public benefit corporation with unusual control terms. A trust of independent trustees elects a majority of our board, and a single Class F share held by our seven co-founders carries 50.1% of the stockholder vote. Amazon and Google hold non-voting Class N stock equal to 14.6% and 13.4% of shares outstanding.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - Revenue grew from $4.7B in Q1 2026 to $17.0B in Q3 2026 with about 5,300 employees, and operating cash flow was $6.6B for the nine months. - Compute expense fell from 172% to 57% of revenue year over year, so each added dollar of capacity is earning more. - $232B of pro forma cash plus a $15.0B undrawn revolver funds several years of the build-out without new equity.

Bear case: - $900.9B of committed spend is about 13 times annualized Q3 revenue. If growth slows, the contracts do not. - Margin already slipped in Q3 (adjusted operating margin 15% versus 19% in Q2) when the company had to buy $5.0B of expensive short-term compute. - Google, Microsoft and Amazon are suppliers, distributors (44% of revenue), shareholders and competitors at once. OpenAI and Google are named as direct rivals. Public holders cannot outvote the founders or elect a board majority.

Key swing factor: Whether revenue keeps growing faster than the compute commitments that come due, starting with the $70.7B owed in the twelve months after 2026-09-30.

1. Business Description

We develop and distribute active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and intermediates, with some contract development work, across more than 35 products in six categories: anti-cancer, anticoagulant, antimalarial, antiviral, bronchodilator and respiratory. We sell direct and through about 100 distributors and partners in more than 35 countries across Europe, Africa, the Middle East, India, Asia and Central and South America, and we make our money on the spread between what contract manufacturers charge us and what drug makers pay us. Revenue was $651.7M and net income $77.7M in the fiscal year to 2026-03-31.

We do not manufacture. At 2026-03-31, 56 contract manufacturers made our products (48 a year earlier), and a plant in Latvia is planned, not built. Property and equipment was $21.8M against $651.7M of revenue. Our balance sheet is working capital: $227.8M of receivables and $301.1M of inventory against $29.0M of cash, so about 81% of a year's revenue is tied up in customers and stock. We are run from Hung Hom, Kowloon, Hong Kong, through Zoar HK; the listed share carries a Cayman Islands ISIN. None of our revenue comes from China.

We listed on NYSE American on 2026-11-18 by merging with Impact BioMedical, a DSS-controlled licensor of wellness and biotech IP with $31k of nine-month revenue. Impact holders received about 4.8% of Zoar. Our founder, chairman, CEO and interim CFO, Kanans Visvanats, holds 167,976,000 of about 178.7M shares, roughly 93%.

Windward Bio Group AG (WWB) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Biotech (respiratory and dermatology antibodies) Market Cap: ~$1.01B at the $17.00 offer (my calculation, below) | Index: none yet (IPO 2026-11-12) Closest comps: UPB, AMGN, AZN, SNY M&A FLAG: None. Sanofi bought $50M of shares and pre-funded warrants in a private placement alongside the IPO.

1. Business Description

We develop long-acting antibodies for asthma, COPD and atopic dermatitis. Our lead drug, WIN378, blocks TSLP, the same target as Tezspire from Amgen and AstraZeneca, and we have engineered it to be dosed twice a year instead of monthly. We have no product revenue; we fund the work with equity, including $175M from this IPO and $50M from Sanofi.

The commercial case rests on dosing interval. Tezspire has shown that blocking TSLP works across severe asthma regardless of eosinophil level, but patients inject it every four weeks. A drug with similar efficacy given every six months would be easier to stay on and easier for a physician to choose. WIN378 is in a Phase 2/3 trial in severe uncontrolled asthma, and we expect the Phase 2 primary results in the first half of 2027. We plan studies in COPD and atopic dermatitis after that.

We are based in Basel. At 06-30-2026 we held $207.3M of cash and marketable securities, before the IPO proceeds. We lost $48.3M in 2025 and $42.6M in the first half of 2026, up from $22.8M in the first half of 2025, as the Phase 2/3 trial enrolled.

What would make us different is the half-life, and that is not proven yet. The twice-yearly schedule is a design goal that the Phase 2 data have to support: the drug must hold TSLP suppression for six months at a dose that can be injected.

3. Investment Debate

vVARDIS Holding AG (VVVV) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: MedTech / Dental Market Cap: ~$0.97B at the $18.00 offer (my estimate, below) | Index: none yet (IPO 2026-11-12) Closest comps: ALGN, XRAY, NVST, STMN.SW M&A FLAG: None.

1. Business Description

We sell Curodont, a liquid that a dentist or hygienist paints onto an early-stage cavity so the enamel regrows and the tooth needs no drilling or filling. Dental practices buy it from us, mostly in the United States, and we earn revenue per unit sold.

Our main product, Curodont Repair Fluoride Plus, is regulated in the United States as an over-the-counter drug on the basis of its sodium fluoride content. That route let us sell it without a new drug approval. The treatment takes a few minutes of chair time and can be applied by a hygienist, so a practice can treat lesions it would otherwise watch until they need a filling.

Growth has been fast from a small base. Net revenue for the nine months to 09-30-2026 was $47.7–49.2M on preliminary figures, against $22.3M a year earlier; nearly all of the increase came from the United States. Gross margin reached 86.4% in the first half of 2026. We still lose money: the nine-month net loss was $39.7–41.3M, about the same as the year before, because sales and marketing spending has grown with revenue.

We are based in Zug, Switzerland. Our founders hold Class B shares that carry one vote each at one-tenth of the par value of a Class A share, which gives them 87.6% of the votes after the offering.

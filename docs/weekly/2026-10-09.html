What that ruling was based on. Buckets 1 and 5 are genuinely discretionary, so they were not opened on the “mandatory anyway” reasoning that covers 2 and 3. Three refusals that override everything above

  • No market cap means no auto-add, ever. The size buckets are gated on it, and for Bucket 1 — which has no floor — it stands in for “does this actually trade yet”. A Form 10 SpinCo has a core SIC code and no shares, so it cannot auto-add on the sector alone. A cap written as prose (~$3.7B) is refused too, and says so rather than claiming the cap is missing.
  • A ticker on the provenance removals list is never auto-added. A vendor has no idea a row was deliberately removed and will keep re-proposing it. An FMP biopharma screen re-proposed ALBT eight days after it was removed for leaving healthcare.
  • Auto means unasked, not unvalidated. Every auto-add routes through the same enrichment gate as a manual one, which refuses a half-filled row and says why.

What happens to your reply

A poller reads #ipo-spinoffs-newissues at 09:20, 13:20 and 18:20 ET. It reads both top-level channel messages and thread replies — where you post makes no difference. It only ever acts on a ticker already sitting pending in the ledger, it ignores everyone but you, and it is idempotent by message timestamp so a transient failure cannot re-fire an approval. It answers in the thread with what landed and what did not, then republishes exports/ so the change reaches the downstream consumers.

TRXB — Treg agonist, Lilly-backed, priced at the bottom

TRex Bio priced 8,333,334 shares at $14.00 on 10-08, the bottom of its $14–16 range, for $116.7M; the deal was not upsized. With 19.1M as-converted shares before the deal, that is ~27.45M shares and ~$384M at the offer. Pro forma cash is ~$194M (est.), so EV is ~$190M. Eli Lilly owned ~24% before the IPO and said it would buy ~$13M of the deal.

Its lead drug, TRB-061 (a TNFR2 agonist that expands regulatory T cells in tissue), is in a Phase 1b trial in atopic dermatitis, with topline data in mid-2027. Lilly runs the partnered TRB-051 and plans a Phase 2a in lupus in 2H26. TRex has received $70M from Lilly so far, with up to $577.5M in milestones still possible. The case against: Lilly has handed back 2 of its 3 programs and Janssen ended its deal in 2025. The closest Treg peer on the sheet is NKTR.

RTSN — a $100M single-asset biotech, added because the rule is against excluding it

Retension priced an upsized 3.75M shares (from 3.3M) at $12.00, the midpoint, for $45M. That gives ~8.33M shares and ~$100M, with ~$53M of cash. Its one asset, RTN-001, is a modified-release PDE-5 inhibitor for uncontrolled hypertension. A 280-patient Phase 2b reads out in 1H27. The pilot data are small and old: in 40 patients on the earlier formulation, systolic blood pressure fell 8.8 mmHg more than placebo.

On the merits I would have excluded it. It lists on the Nasdaq Capital Market, its auditors flagged going-concern doubt before the IPO, its FY2025 financials were restated, the composition-of-matter patent expires in 2027, and it competes with covered MLYS, ALNY and AZN, each of which has a larger dataset. The Bucket 1 ruling (2026-08-09, reaffirmed on BLSM) is any size with no floor, so it is in. Reply remove RTSN if you agree with the case against.

Company Summaries

TRex Bio, Inc. (TRXB)

We make drugs that switch on regulatory T cells, the immune system's own brakes, inside inflamed skin and gut tissue, instead of suppressing immunity body-wide. Our lead drug, TRB-061, is an injectable TNFR2 agonist in a Phase 1b atopic dermatitis trial, with data due in mid-2027. Eli Lilly licensed a third program (TRB-051) and has paid us $70M so far. We are pre-revenue and fund ourselves from the $116.7M IPO and partner milestones.

Retension Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (RTSN)

We are developing RTN-001, a once-daily pill for patients whose blood pressure stays high despite two to five existing drugs. It is a PDE-5 inhibitor (Viagra's class) redesigned to relax the large central arteries; a 280-patient Phase 2b reports in the first half of 2027. We are pre-revenue, own one asset licensed from Kadmon (now Sanofi) at a low-teens royalty, and raised $45M in our IPO.

TRex Bio, Inc. (TRXB) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Biotech Market Cap: ~$0.38B | Index: Pre-index (IPO 2026-10-09) Closest comps: NKTR, ANAB (Treg biology); IL-2 and OX40 programs in atopic dermatitis M&A FLAG: None. Partner retrenchment in the last 15 months: Janssen (J&J) terminated its 2021 collaboration for convenience in July 2025 (all licenses ended October 2025), and Lilly has handed back 2 of the 3 programs in its 2023 deal, keeping only TRB-051.

1. Business Description

We make drugs that switch on the immune system's own brakes, regulatory T cells (Tregs), inside inflamed skin and gut tissue, rather than suppressing the immune system everywhere. Our lead drug, TRB-061, is an injectable protein that activates TNFR2, a receptor found on the most active Tregs in tissue. It is in a Phase 1b trial in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, with topline data expected in mid-2027. We raised $116.7M in our IPO at $14 per share, which funds us into the second half of 2029 (per S-1).

Our science starts with fresh human tissue, not mouse models. Our "Deep Biology" platform maps how Tregs behave in skin, gut and lung tissue taken from patients, and we pick targets from that map. TRB-061 is a TNFR2 agonist Fc-fusion protein. The Phase 1a healthy-volunteer portion started in June 2025. In biomarker work from that trial, Tregs expanded, showed markers of a more stable, longer-lived, tissue-homing state, and produced more of the calming cytokine IL-10. The Phase 1b portion in atopic dermatitis patients started in April 2026. It measures safety, drug levels and skin biomarkers, and includes exploratory efficacy endpoints. Our second wholly owned drug, TRB-071, is an antibody that activates CD30 on tissue Tregs and also blocks CD30 ligand, which drives inflammation. Human genetics link both CD30 and its ligand to inflammatory bowel disease. TRB-071 is finishing IND-enabling work (a 3-month monkey toxicology study is underway), and we plan to start a Phase 1 trial in the first half of 2027. With the IPO proceeds we plan to run Phase 2 trials of TRB-061 in atopic dermatitis and alopecia areata through topline data, and Phase 1 for TRB-071.

Our partnerships pay us without diluting our own pipeline. Under our January 2023 deal, Eli Lilly paid us $55.0M upfront and a $15.0M development milestone in July 2024. On the one remaining program, TRB-051, we can earn up to $70.0M more in development milestones, $157.5M in commercial milestones and $350.0M in sales milestones, plus 6–10% tiered royalties. Lilly started a Phase 1 trial of TRB-051 in June 2024 and has said it plans to start a Phase 2a trial in lupus with skin involvement in the second half of 2026. Janssen paid us a $1.0M option fee in 2024 before ending that deal in 2025. Lilly is our largest shareholder at about 24% before the offering. J&J's venture arm, Pfizer Ventures, SV Health and Alexandria also own stakes.

We had $90.9M in cash and marketable securities on June 30, 2026, and no debt. Adding net proceeds of about $104M (est.), we have roughly $194M pro forma. We lost $34.8M in 2025 and $29.3M in the first half of 2026, as R&D spending stepped up for the Phase 1b. Two things set us apart. The first is the mechanism: approved drugs for atopic dermatitis, such as dupilumab and the JAK inhibitors, block inflammatory signals and must be taken indefinitely. Activating Tregs aims to rebuild the tissue's own control of inflammation, which could make remissions last after treatment stops. No approved drug has shown that. The second is selectivity: we target the TNFR2+ Tregs that live in tissue, not every Treg in the blood, which is the bet that separates us from the IL-2-based Treg programs.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - Lilly has validated the platform twice: $70M in cash already received, and up to $577.5M in remaining milestones plus 6–10% royalties on TRB-051, which is heading into a Phase 2a in lupus in 2H26. On a ~$190M EV, the market is paying very little for two wholly owned clinical programs. - If a Treg agonist delivers durable remission in atopic dermatitis, it would be a different product category from the chronic blockers (dupilumab, the OX40/OX40L antibodies). Atopic dermatitis affects about 16M US adults and about 225M people worldwide (per S-1). - About $194M pro forma cash funds operations into 2H 2029, past the mid-2027 Phase 1b readout and through Phase 2 topline. Near-term financing risk is low.

Bear case: - Two of three Lilly programs and the entire Janssen deal have been handed back, so partners have pruned this platform as often as they have endorsed it. The deal priced at the bottom of the range. - The Treg space is crowded and has a history of misses. IL-2-based Treg drugs from Amgen (efavaleukin), Lilly/Nektar and others produced mixed or failed results. Nektar's rezpegaldesleukin is ahead in atopic dermatitis. The S-1 also names "several companies" in the TNFR2 class (not identified). The approved standard of care (dupilumab, lebrikizumab, JAK inhibitors) sets a high efficacy bar. - The Phase 1b readout is mainly safety and biomarker data with exploratory efficacy. A clean but uninterpretable readout in mid-2027 would leave the core question open until Phase 2.

Key swing factor: Whether the mid-2027 Phase 1b atopic dermatitis data show a skin-level Treg effect that translates into clinical response (EASI/itch), ideally lasting after dosing stops. That durability is the whole differentiation thesis.

Retension Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (RTSN) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Biotech Market Cap: ~$0.10B | Index: Pre-index (IPO 2026-10-09) Closest comps: MLYS, ALNY, AZN M&A FLAG: None

1. Business Description

We are developing RTN-001, a once-daily pill for people whose blood pressure stays high even on two to five blood-pressure drugs. RTN-001 belongs to the same drug class as Viagra (PDE-5 inhibitors), but it was redesigned to reach the large central arteries and relax them, rather than acting mainly on small, thin-walled vessels. A 280-patient Phase 2b dose-ranging trial started in October 2025 and should report initial data in the first half of 2027. We raised $45M in an upsized IPO at $12 per share.

RTN-001 is our only drug, so the company rises or falls with it. It began at Surface Logix as SLx-2101, then passed through Nano Terra and Kadmon, which Sanofi acquired in 2021. Our founders took it back in November 2023 through a sublicense from Redux, their affiliated entity. The mechanism is the nitric-oxide/cGMP pathway, which the body uses to relax artery walls. PDE-5 breaks down cGMP, and blocking it keeps arteries relaxed. Earlier PDE-5 drugs lower blood pressure only modestly, because they bind tightly to tissue and do not reach the central arterial smooth muscle at useful free-drug levels. Nine completed trials, including two Phase 2 pilots (07-05 and 07-08), make up our existing data. In Trial 07-08, RTN-001 cut systolic blood pressure by a statistically significant 8.8 mmHg on average over eight hours versus placebo (p=0.0019), with a peak placebo-adjusted drop of 12.8 mmHg (p<0.0001). Published figures for vardenafil (Levitra) show 2–5 mmHg. Both pilots used an older immediate-release formulation, were small (Trial 07-08 had 40 patients in a crossover design) and were not powered for significance. No treatment-related serious adverse events occurred. The Phase 2b uses a new modified-release formulation. Its primary endpoint is the change in office-seated systolic blood pressure at week 4 versus placebo, and its key secondary endpoints are 24-hour ambulatory and central blood pressure.

Our economics are simple. We owe Kadmon (Sanofi) a low-teens percentage royalty on net sales and on sublicense revenue. Our CEO, Eric Keller, and CSO, Paul Sweetnam, would receive part of those royalties as former Surface Logix securityholders. Kadmon holds a right of first negotiation on any commercial partnership. The licensed composition-of-matter patent expires in 2027. Protection to at least 2044 depends on a crystalline-polymorph patent we were granted in March 2025, a thinner moat than composition of matter. We had $13.5M in cash on June 30, 2026, against a 2025 net loss of $6.3M and a first-half 2026 net loss of $7.5M. After the IPO we have about $53M (est.). We have earmarked about $18M to finish the Phase 2b and about $10M to prepare for Phase 3. The S-1 projects at least 21 months of runway (based on the pre-upsize deal), and our auditors had issued a going-concern explanation before the IPO.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - The enterprise value is ~$47M for a Phase 2b asset in a very large indication: about 120M US adults have hypertension (per S-1), and uncontrolled hypertension on 2+ drugs is a sizeable subset. The pilot data showed an 8.8 mmHg average and 12.8 mmHg peak placebo-adjusted systolic drop on top of existing therapy. The S-1 notes that drops as small as 5 mmHg improve cardiovascular outcomes. - The PDE-5 class is well understood and generic, so safety risk is lower than for new mechanisms. A once-daily oral pill could fit on top of standard combination regimens. - The bar for being "interesting" is low: a clean dose response in the 280-patient Phase 2b in 1H27 would re-rate a ~$100M company, and the cash funds the trial to readout.

Bear case: - The field is competitive and well funded. Aldosterone synthase inhibitors (AstraZeneca's baxdrostat, Mineralys's lorundrostat), Alnylam/Roche's twice-yearly siRNA zilebesiran, and Idorsia's approved endothelin antagonist aprocitentan all target resistant or uncontrolled hypertension with larger datasets. A drug from a cheap, generic class will struggle on price and payer access. - The supporting data are old, small, short-duration and based on a different (immediate-release) formulation. The 24-hour reduction at 10 mg missed significance (p=0.0532). Lasting 24-hour control with the modified-release pill is unproven. The company has one asset, a pre-IPO going-concern explanation, and patent protection after 2027 that rests on a polymorph patent. - Insiders sit on both sides of the royalty chain (the Redux and Surface Logix interests of the CEO and CSO). The deal is also small, about $45M, with a thin float of ~3.75M new shares and a Nasdaq Capital Market listing, so the stock is likely to be illiquid and volatile.

Key swing factor: Whether the modified-release RTN-001 produces a placebo-adjusted office systolic reduction of ~8–10 mmHg or more at week 4, with 24-hour ambulatory support, in the 1H 2027 Phase 2b. Anything less leaves it behind the aldosterone synthase inhibitors.

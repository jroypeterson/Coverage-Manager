Three refusals that override everything above

  • No market cap means no auto-add, ever. The size buckets are gated on it, and for Bucket 1 — which has no floor — it stands in for “does this actually trade yet”. A Form 10 SpinCo has a core SIC code and no shares, so it cannot auto-add on the sector alone. A cap written as prose (~$3.7B) is refused too, and says so rather than claiming the cap is missing.
  • A ticker on the provenance removals list is never auto-added. A vendor has no idea a row was deliberately removed and will keep re-proposing it. An FMP biopharma screen re-proposed ALBT eight days after it was removed for leaving healthcare.
  • Auto means unasked, not unvalidated. Every auto-add routes through the same enrichment gate as a manual one, which refuses a half-filled row and says why.

What happens to your reply

A poller reads #ipo-spinoffs-newissues at 09:20, 13:20 and 18:20 ET. It reads both top-level channel messages and thread replies — where you post makes no difference. It only ever acts on a ticker already sitting pending in the ledger, it ignores everyone but you, and it is idempotent by message timestamp so a transient failure cannot re-fire an approval. It answers in the thread with what landed and what did not, then republishes exports/ so the change reaches the downstream consumers.

Company Summaries

Agility Robotics, Inc. (AGLT)

We build Digit, a bipedal humanoid robot that moves totes and materials in warehouses and factories. Our named customers include GXO, Schaeffler and Toyota Canada. We keep ownership of deployed robots rather than selling them outright. Our next-generation Digit reaches early-access customers in 1H 2027, with general availability targeted for the end of 2027.

Bambusa Therapeutics, Inc. (BBTX)

We develop bispecific antibodies for inflammatory disease. Our lead, BBT001, hits IL-4Rα (the Dupixent target) and IL-31 (itch) in one molecule for atopic dermatitis, and showed a placebo-adjusted EASI benefit from week 1 in a July 2026 proof-of-concept study. We bought the asset from BioNTech's Chinese subsidiary. We are pre-revenue, lost $41.1M in 2025, and say the IPO funds us into late 2028.

Aspen Neuroscience, Inc. (ASPE)

We turn a Parkinson's patient's own skin cells into dopamine neurons and transplant them back into the brain. Because the cells are the patient's own, the therapy should need no immunosuppression. Sasineprocel is in the Phase 1/2a ASPIRO trial. We lost $62.1M in 2025, and our S-1 says the IPO money will not carry us through Phase 3.

Nordicus Partners Corporation (NORD)

We hold three small Nordic drug-development subsidiaries: Orocidin (QR-01, periodontitis), Bio-Convert (QR-02, oral leukoplakia) and NoviThera (QR-04, psoriasis). None has reached a Phase 2 start; we plan Phase 2a trials in Europe in 1H 2027. We have only nominal revenue and lost $4.0M in the year to March 2026.

Our lead, BBT001, binds IL-4Rα, the Dupixent target that drives type 2 inflammation, and IL-31, the cytokine behind itch, on the view that atopic dermatitis patients care about the itch as much as the rash. We bought it from BioNTech's Chinese subsidiary (formerly Biotheus), which is why our trials run in both the West and China. BBT001 is in a global Phase 1 in the US, Australia, Europe and New Zealand and a Phase 1a/1b in China, plus a Phase 2a in chronic spontaneous urticaria in China. Our second program, BBT002, pairs IL-4Rα with IL-5 for COPD and nasal polyps.

In July 2026 we reported proof-of-concept data in bio-naïve AD patients after four weeks of IV dosing: a statistically significant placebo-adjusted EASI reduction from week 1. We plan to start a Phase 2b in AD by year-end 2026, with more readouts through 1H27. We have no revenue. We lost $41.1M in 2025 and $22.4M in 1H26, and our backers include RA Capital, BVF, KKR's Dawn Biopharma and ATHOS.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - IL-4Rα is the most validated target in dermatology; Dupixent sells >$15B a year. Adding itch control via IL-31 (nemolizumab's mechanism) in one molecule is a clean differentiation argument. - Week-1 onset in the PoC study is the right early signal; speed of itch relief is where Dupixent leaves room. - Two programs in different markets (AD, COPD/CRSwNP) give two shots off one platform.

Bear case: - The AD bispecific field is crowded: Apogee, Sanofi's own follow-ons, and many Chinese bispecifics licensed West. A ~$0.6B EV may be pricing an asset that will be one of ten. - Four-week IV data in a small PoC says little about a subcutaneous, quarterly-dosing commercial profile, which is the bar now. - China-origin assets carry BIOSECURE-style political risk, and part of the data package comes from Chinese trials.

Key swing factor: whether the Phase 2b shows EASI and itch efficacy on a dosing schedule competitive with long-acting IL-13 and OX40 programs.

1. Business Description

We make a personalised cell therapy for Parkinson's disease. We take a skin biopsy from the patient, reprogram the cells into induced pluripotent stem cells, grow them into the dopamine-producing neurons the disease destroys, and implant them into the patient's brain. Because the cells are the patient's own, we expect no need for lifelong immunosuppression. That is the main mechanical difference from the off-the-shelf approach Bayer's BlueRock is taking with bemdaneprocel. Our lead, sasineprocel, is in the Phase 1/2a ASPIRO trial in mid-stage, "moderately advanced" Parkinson's.

We are pre-revenue and spend about $50–60M a year: a net loss of $62.1M in 2025 and $36.5M in 1H26, with $72.4M of cash at 6/30/26. Our own S-1 says the IPO money plus existing cash will not be enough to finish ASPIRO or the planned Phase 3, so we will need to raise again. The deal priced at the bottom of the $15–17 range for $140.6M gross.

Three refusals that override everything above

  • No market cap means no auto-add, ever. The size buckets are gated on it, and for Bucket 1 — which has no floor — it stands in for “does this actually trade yet”. A Form 10 SpinCo has a core SIC code and no shares, so it cannot auto-add on the sector alone. A cap written as prose (~$3.7B) is refused too, and says so rather than claiming the cap is missing.
  • A ticker on the provenance removals list is never auto-added. A vendor has no idea a row was deliberately removed and will keep re-proposing it. An FMP biopharma screen re-proposed ALBT eight days after it was removed for leaving healthcare.
  • Auto means unasked, not unvalidated. Every auto-add routes through the same enrichment gate as a manual one, which refuses a half-filled row and says why.

DAVION HEALTHCARE PLC (DAVI) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: MedTech / Consumer home-screening diagnostics Market Cap: ~$0.29B (25.0M shares × $11.65, 10-22 close) | Index: none (direct listing 10-22) Closest comps: QDEL, MYGN, GH (none is a close business match) M&A FLAG: None. 1. Business Description

We develop non-invasive home screening kits that let a consumer check for a possible abnormality before seeing a doctor. BreastCheck and Testic look for breast and testicular lumps, FootFlow for poor blood flow in the feet, and ThermaDerm uses skin temperature. We do not manufacture, run trials or sell directly. R&D is contracted to third parties, manufacturing is outsourced, and we plan to earn licensing income, starting with a global BreastCheck licence to NeuRX Health. BreastCheck was due to launch in the US in 1H26, with FootFlow about six months behind. We have since announced a UK distribution platform, which moves us toward selling directly.

The scale is small. We had no revenue through June 2025 and no employees at the F-1 filing. Our balance sheet is almost entirely intangibles: €65.0M of identifiable intangible assets against €4,396 of cash at 06-30-25. Our day-to-day costs are met by non-interest-bearing advances from Malbrite Ltd, a company controlled by our CEO, Jack Kaye, who is now moving to Chairman as Glenn Williams takes over as CEO. Our listing on 10-22 was a direct listing of 25.0M existing shares, so we raised no money.

What makes us different is hard to argue mechanically. BreastCheck and FootFlow are Class I devices registered by self-declaration with the FDA, UK and EU regulators. That keeps cost low, but it also means there is no clearance or clinical-validation moat to stop a competitor.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - A $300M cap on a consumer screening franchise. If BreastCheck reaches retail through NeuRX and the UK platform, licensing revenue carries almost no cost of goods. - A new CEO and a new UK distribution strategy suggest management is moving from licensing-only to direct sales.

Bear case: - There is no cash, no revenue and no capital raised. The company depends on advances from a company its CEO controls, so any operating plan needs dilutive financing soon. - €65M of intangibles underpin ~100% of book equity, and the 2023–24 statements were restated with material weaknesses. Book value is not a floor. - The products are Class I self-declared devices with no clinical moat. Revenue rests on one licensee.

Key swing factor: whether BreastCheck generates any real licensing revenue before the company has to raise money.

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Biotech — extracellular targeted protein degradation Market Cap: ~$0.99B at the $18 IPO price (~55.0M post-IPO shares) | Index: Pre-IPO → Nasdaq 10-23 Closest comps: KYMR, ARVN, GLUE, NRIX (degraders); JSPR (allergy/mast cell) M&A FLAG: None

1. Business Description

We make drugs that remove harmful proteins from outside the cell, which the intracellular degraders (Kymera, Arvinas) cannot reach. Our molecules bind a circulating target, such as IgE or an autoantibody, and tag it for uptake and destruction by liver receptors. The approach comes from Nobel laureate Carolyn Bertozzi's LYTAC research at Stanford, and she co-founded the company. Antibodies such as Xolair block IgE; we aim to eliminate it, which in principle gives deeper suppression and less frequent dosing.

We have three named programs. LCA-0061, the lead, is an antibody–small-molecule conjugate against IgE and is in Phase 1 in people with elevated IgE, including food-allergy patients. LCA-0062 is a fast-follower IgE degrader in IND-enabling work. LCA-0321 targets TSH-receptor autoantibodies in Graves' disease and is in Phase 1. Data from LCA-0061 and LCA-0321 are expected in 2027, and initial LCA-0062 data in 2H27.

We have no product revenue. We earned collaboration revenue from a 2021 Eli Lilly deal ($35M upfront), which ran off to zero in 1H26. We raised $267M of venture capital, led by Versant (~19% pre-IPO), and now ~$318M in the IPO. With $138M of cash at 09-30-26, we guide our runway into 2H29.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - IgE depletion rather than blockade is a differentiated route into a ~$5B+ anti-IgE category that Xolair defines. Phase 1 IgE knockdown is a binary, measurable early readout. - The upsized pricing at the top of the range points to real institutional demand. Runway into 2H29 covers both 2027 readouts without new money. - The platform should extend to other circulating targets (autoantibodies in Graves'), giving multiple shots on one mechanism.

Bear case: - Every program is Phase 1, with the first meaningful data in 2027. The ~$0.56B EV is paid for a platform, not a product. - Liver-receptor uptake at therapeutic doses is unproven at scale. Safety from heavy hepatic clearance is the obvious risk. - Competing IgE and allergy approaches (Xolair biosimilars, mast-cell programs like JSPR, anti-IgE antibodies with longer half-lives) set a high bar for clinical differentiation.

Key swing factor: whether LCA-0061 shows deeper and more durable IgE depletion than omalizumab in Phase 1.

FORGE NANO HOLDINGS, INC. (NANO) — Quick Background

1. Business Description

We make atomic layer deposition (ALD) systems and the coatings they apply. ALD builds a film one atomic layer at a time, which is how chipmakers coat the deep, narrow structures in advanced logic, memory and packaging. We sell tools such as the TEPHRA platform, including a 200mm system launched in September 2025, to chip and photonics manufacturers. We also run a materials business, Atomic Armor, that coats battery cathode and anode powders so cells last longer and run safer. Our customers there include commercial battery makers and defense programs, and Samsung SDI is both investor and partner.

We are early commercially. A third-party tear sheet quoting our investor update puts 2025 at ~$14.5M of net sales plus grant income, of which $6.4M was grants, so ~$8M of product and service sales. The SPAC deal valued us at ~$1.2B pre-money and ~$1.6B post-money with no redemptions. It raised a $123M PIPE plus up to ~$244M in trust, though actual redemptions are not yet disclosed. We are based in Colorado and position ourselves as US-made equipment for onshored chip and battery supply chains.

Our edge, where it exists, is process know-how. ALD tools are a concentrated market (ASM International, Lam, Applied, Tokyo Electron), and our pitch is high-throughput ALD for powders and specialty substrates, which incumbent wafer tools do not serve.

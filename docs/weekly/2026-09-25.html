What that ruling was based on. Buckets 1 and 5 are genuinely discretionary, so they were not opened on the “mandatory anyway” reasoning that covers 2 and 3. Three refusals that override everything above

  • No market cap means no auto-add, ever. The size buckets are gated on it, and for Bucket 1 — which has no floor — it stands in for “does this actually trade yet”. A Form 10 SpinCo has a core SIC code and no shares, so it cannot auto-add on the sector alone. A cap written as prose (~$3.7B) is refused too, and says so rather than claiming the cap is missing.
  • A ticker on the provenance removals list is never auto-added. A vendor has no idea a row was deliberately removed and will keep re-proposing it. An FMP biopharma screen re-proposed ALBT eight days after it was removed for leaving healthcare.
  • Auto means unasked, not unvalidated. Every auto-add routes through the same enrichment gate as a manual one, which refuses a half-filled row and says why.

What happens to your reply

Company Summaries

Electra Therapeutics, Inc. (ETRA)

We develop antibodies that kill the specific immune cells driving a runaway inflammatory response instead of suppressing the whole immune system. Our lead drug, ipsoprubart, targets SIRP proteins on overactive myeloid and T cells, and we are testing it first in secondary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis — a frequently fatal cytokine storm with no FDA-approved treatment — in a Phase 2/3 trial that completes enrollment in 2H27. We have no revenue; IPO proceeds fund the program to an FDA filing, with runway guided into 2029.

ADARx Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (ADRX)

We make siRNA drugs that silence the gene behind a disease so the harmful protein is never made. Our lead, onvuzosiran, shuts down liver production of plasma kallikrein to prevent hereditary angioedema attacks and is in Phase 3 (topline by end-2027); behind it sit agazisiran for complement-driven kidney and eye disease and a Factor XI program for stroke prevention. We have no product revenue — AbbVie paid $335M upfront in 2025 to license our platform and owes milestones and royalties.

Ligent Technologies, Inc. (9856.HK)

We make the optical transceivers that convert electrical signals to light and back inside AI data centers and telecom networks, plus the optical chips inside them and fiber-to-the-home terminals. About 70% of revenue is datacom modules sold to cloud operators and network-equipment vendors, and we ship 800G and 1.6T parts in volume. 2025 revenue was RMB 8.36B, up 64%, with RMB 873M of profit; Hisense Group is our controlling shareholder.

Electra Therapeutics, Inc. (ETRA) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Biotech — precision immunology Market Cap: ~$0.74B at $11.82 (~$0.94B at the $15.00 offer) | Index: Newly public (Nasdaq, 2026-09-18) Closest comps: SOBI.ST, ARGX, DNLI M&A FLAG: None

1. Business Description

We build antibodies that remove the specific immune cells causing a disease and leave the rest of the immune system alone. The standard answer to a runaway immune response is broad suppression — steroids, etoposide, cytokine blockers — which dampens everything and leaves patients exposed to infection. Our bet is that you can instead find the cells driving the fire and delete them.

Our handle for doing that is a family of receptors called signal regulatory proteins (SIRPs), which are expressed on activated myeloid cells and certain T cells. Our lead antibody, ipsoprubart, binds across the SIRP family and depletes the cells carrying it. We are developing it first in secondary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (sHLH), a hyperinflammatory syndrome triggered by cancer, infection or immunotherapy in which macrophages and T cells flood the body with cytokines and patients die of multi-organ failure. There is no FDA-approved drug for sHLH. In a Phase 1b in malignancy-associated HLH we reported 100% overall survival and response at eight weeks — small numbers, but in a disease where the historical mortality is the headline. A Phase 2/3 in treatment-naïve sHLH is enrolling, with enrollment completion guided for the second half of 2027. Behind it we run a Phase 1 in T-cell and NK-cell malignancies (data 2H27) and a second antibody, ELA822, heading into Phase 1/1b.

We have no revenue. We had $97.7M of cash at 30 June 2026 and raised $350M gross in the IPO (23.3M shares at $15.00, upsized, priced at the midpoint), which we have allocated as roughly $220M to take ipsoprubart through an FDA submission, $25M to the malignancy trial and $50M to ELA822, with runway guided into 2029. Westlake BioPartners (10.4% post-IPO) and OrbiMed (9.1%) are the largest holders; the fall-2025 $183M Series C was co-led by Nextech and EQT Life Sciences.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - An orphan indication with no approved therapy and very high mortality; a clean Phase 2/3 win is close to a de facto standard of care, and sHLH also occurs as a CAR-T / bispecific toxicity, which grows with the oncology cell-therapy market. - Depletion via SIRP is a platform claim: if it works in sHLH, the same mechanism reads into macrophage activation syndrome, T/NK-cell lymphomas and other cytokine-storm settings. - Fully funded to an FDA submission on IPO proceeds, and trading at an EV of roughly $0.3B after a 21% slide from the offer.

Bear case: - The efficacy evidence is a small Phase 1b; sHLH trials are heterogeneous (triggers differ) and hard to enroll, so the 2H27 enrollment guide can slip. - Depleting immune cells in already-sick patients carries infection risk; a safety signal would end the "precision" argument. - Sobi's Gamifant (emapalumab) is approved in primary HLH and MAS and is used off-label; Sobi (covered SOBI.ST) can run a label-expansion trial with a commercial product in hand.

ADARx Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (ADRX) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Biotech — RNA interference Market Cap: ~$1.74B at the $17.00 offer | Index: Newly public (Nasdaq Global Select, 2026-09-25) Closest comps: ALNY, ARWR, IONS, PHVS M&A FLAG: None (AbbVie collaboration, May 2025 — a license, not an acquisition)

1. Business Description

We make siRNA drugs: short double-stranded RNAs that ride into a cell, find the messenger RNA for one protein and destroy it, so the protein is never made. The category was proven by Alnylam and is now a mainstream modality; our claim is a better version of the chemistry (our "Messenger RNA Silencing Technology") and delivery beyond the liver ("Cell Targeted Delivery").

Our lead drug, onvuzosiran, silences plasma kallikrein in the liver to prevent attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE), the rare genetic disease in which patients suffer unpredictable, sometimes airway-threatening swelling. Early data showed up to a 93% reduction in plasma kallikrein, which in HAE is the variable that matters; our Phase 3 (STOP-HAE) reports topline data by the end of 2027. The pitch is dosing: an siRNA can suppress the target for months, so a patient could inject a few times a year rather than take a daily pill (Pharvaris, covered PHVS, and KalVista) or a frequent injectable. Behind it, agazisiran silences complement factor B — a "pipeline in a product" for complement-driven kidney disease, geographic atrophy and PNH, with Phase 2 data mid-to-late 2027 — and ADX-626 targets Factor XI for secondary stroke prevention. ADX-077 (obesity) and ADX-199 (Alzheimer's) are preclinical and carry the extrahepatic claim.

We have no product revenue. Our funding is AbbVie — which paid $335M upfront in May 2025 to license the platform for neurology, immunology and oncology targets, with up to $385M in option payments and up to $7.45B in milestones plus royalties — and equity: $427.3M of cash at 30 June 2026 before the IPO, plus $446.3M gross from 26.25M shares at $17.00 (upsized, top of range), and ~$89M from AbbVie in a concurrent placement that takes it to ~4.9%. Our co-founders, CEO Dr. Zhen Li and CTO Dr. Rui Zhu (formerly running siRNA programs at Arrowhead), built the platform.

Ligent Technologies, Inc. (9856.HK) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Tech / Optical Interconnect Market Cap: ~$4.3B (HK$34.48 close on 2026-09-22) | Index: Newly public (HKEX, 2026-09-22) Closest comps: 3308.HK, COHR, LITE, AAOI, FN M&A FLAG: None identified

1. Business Description

We make optical transceivers: the pluggable modules at each end of a fiber link that turn electrical signals from a switch or GPU server into light and back. Inside an AI data center every GPU cluster needs thousands of them, and each generation (400G → 800G → 1.6T) roughly doubles the bandwidth per module and resets pricing. About 70% of our revenue now comes from these datacom modules, sold to cloud operators and to the network-equipment vendors that build their switches; the rest is telecom access gear — fiber-to-the-home optical network terminals — and the optical chips we design and fabricate in-house.

We began as Hisense's optical-communications arm (the relationship dates to 2002; the company was set up in 2009) and are still controlled by Hisense Group, which retains roughly 40% after the IPO. We are headquartered in San Jose. The business went from a diversified telecom-optics manufacturer to an AI-datacenter supplier inside two years: data-center transceiver sales rose 36.6% to RMB 3.74B in the first half of 2026, and we are in volume production of 800G and 1.6T modules. Owning our laser and detector chips is the part that is mechanically differentiating — it is where the margin in a transceiver sits and where supply was tight in 2025 — but that chip line is still loss-making.

We sell hardware by the unit on supply agreements, so revenue follows data-center capex and each product generation's ramp. The IPO raised HK$5.67B (~$723M) at HK$32.96, with nearly 30 cornerstone investors taking 47% of the book; proceeds go to capacity and AI-related product development.

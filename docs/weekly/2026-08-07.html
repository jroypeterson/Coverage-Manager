Latigo Biotherapeutics, Inc. (LTGO)

We are a clinical-stage pharmaceutical company developing non-opioid pain medicines that block pain signals at the peripheral nerve rather than in the brain, so they relieve pain without the addiction and sedation risk that define opioids. Our approach is to selectively inhibit Nav1.8, a sodium channel expressed almost exclusively on pain-sensing neurons — our lead candidate LTG-001 is an oral inhibitor for acute pain with Phase 2 results published in the New England Journal of Medicine, and LTG-305 is a follow-on in Phase 1 aimed at chronic pain. We have no product revenue and fund ourselves from equity: roughly $321.5M raised privately since 2018, including a $150M Series B led by Blue Owl Capital in March 2025 with Sanofi Ventures, Qatar Investment Authority and Cormorant participating, plus $345.6M gross from this offering. We will make money by selling a branded oral analgesic into the acute-pain market that Vertex's Journavx opened commercially.

Fabrinet (FN)

We are the contract manufacturer the optical-networking industry outsources its hardest hardware to: we build the transceivers, lasers and photonic assemblies that our customers design but do not want to make themselves, mostly out of large campuses in Thailand. We make money on manufacturing services — our customers own the designs and the end-customer relationships, and we earn a margin on precision assembly and test that requires capital and process expertise they would rather not carry. Optical communications is 73.2% of our revenue, and in our fiscal Q3 2026 we delivered a record $1.214B of revenue, up 39% year over year, with data-center interconnect alone up 90.4% to $196.9M. We have launched two datacom transceiver programs with a hyperscale customer and several merchant transceiver programs for data-center scale-out that begin production in early fiscal 2027.

ZHONGJI INNOLIGHT CO., LTD. (3308.HK / 300308.SZ) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Tech / Optical Interconnect (high-speed optical transceivers) Market Cap: ~US$167B (HK$1.30T) | Index: Shenzhen ChiNext; HKEX H-share since 2026-07-30 Closest comps: COHR, LITE, FN, AAOI (none currently in the sheet); in-sheet adjacents 2475.HK, NVDA, TSM M&A FLAG: None. The July 2026 event was a capital raise — an H-share secondary listing — not a change of control.

1. Business Description

We make the optical transceivers that carry data between the GPUs, servers and switches inside AI data centers. Every time a hyperscaler builds a training cluster, the copper that used to connect racks runs out of reach, and the connection becomes a laser firing down a fibre with a module at each end converting electrons to photons and back. We build those modules — a portfolio spanning 10G through 400G, 800G and now 1.6T — and we sell them to Nvidia, Google, Meta and Huawei, plus the networking OEMs that assemble their systems.

The economics are a race, not an annuity. Each speed generation is a fresh qualification cycle: the first supplier to prove it can ship a new module at volume captures the design win and the fat margin, and margin compresses as competitors qualify behind us. We have won that race three years running — LightCounting places us first in global transceiver shipments for a third consecutive year at 23.4% share in 2025, roughly seven points ahead of Coherent at ~16% — and our 2025 global optical-interconnect revenue share was 21.2%. In the generation that matters right now we are further ahead than the headline share suggests: we are currently the only company globally mass-producing and delivering both 800G and 1.6T modules at scale, and our position in 1.6T is roughly 50–70%. We hold over 50% of Nvidia's optical-module wallet and are a primary supplier into Google's AI infrastructure.

Scale followed. 2025 revenue was RMB 38.24B (~US$5.6B), up 60% year over year, with net profit up 109%. The 2026 ramp is steeper still: Q1 2026 revenue of RMB 19.5B, up 192% year over year, net profit RMB 5.74B, up 262%, at a record 46% gross margin — margin expanding into a volume ramp, which is the signature of a supplier with pricing power in a shortage.

What genuinely differentiates us is manufacturing readiness at the front edge of the speed curve rather than any single patent. Optical modules are a precision-assembly problem — laser alignment, thermal management, yield at volume — and the constraint is how fast a line can be qualified and scaled, not whether the design exists. Being first to 1.6T at scale is why more than half of the single largest buyer's spend routes through us.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case - Sole-source position at the leading edge: the only vendor mass-producing 800G and 1.6T at scale, ~50–70% share of 1.6T and >50% of Nvidia's optical-module spend. Gross margin hit a record 46% in Q1 2026 while volume nearly tripled — pricing power, not just volume. - The demand driver is capex already committed. Optical module count scales with GPU count and with network topology, and the 800G→1.6T transition resets the content-per-rack higher. - 23.4% shipment share, seven points clear of the #2, sustained three consecutive years — this is a durable lead, not a single-cycle win.

Bear case - US Department of Defense 1260H listing. Innolight appears on the Pentagon's list of Chinese military-linked companies. That is a live and escalating constraint on US customer relationships and on US-person investability, and it is the risk that could break the Nvidia and Google revenue lines irrespective of how good the product is. - Customer concentration is extreme and undisclosed. More than half of one customer's wallet, in a business where that customer is also funding second sources (Coherent, Lumentum, Fabrinet-built merchant programs) precisely to avoid this dependency. - Margin at 46% in a component business is a peak-cycle number, not a steady state. Every prior optical speed transition ended in commoditisation once three or more suppliers qualified; the question is when, not whether.

Key swing factor: whether US restrictions on a 1260H-listed supplier tighten enough to force Nvidia and Google to re-source 1.6T volume — which would hit the highest-margin revenue first.

4. What to Watch

  • H1 2026 interim results (Aug–Sept 2026) — the first report as a dual-listed issuer; watch whether the 46% gross margin holds as 1.6T volume scales.
  • Any expansion of 1260H consequences (procurement bans, export-control actions) — the single largest binary in the story.

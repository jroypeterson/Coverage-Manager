The rules — what adds itself, what waits for you

Four of the five inclusion buckets now enter the universe without asking. Only Bucket 4 — and any New candidate trigger — still waits for your reply, because both are judgement calls by construction.

BucketRuleThresholdBehaviourWhy
2Any IPO or direct listing, any sector ≥ $25BAUTO Mandatory by rule — queueing it just puts a step between the rule and the outcome.

What that ruling was based on. Buckets 1 and 5 are genuinely discretionary, so they were not opened on the “mandatory anyway” reasoning that covers 2 and 3. Replayed over every candidate the lane has ever proposed — 31 names, 29 approved, 2 declined — they auto-add 10 names you approved and neither of the two you declined. Both declines (DPC Industrials, EROC Energy) fall in neither bucket.

Three refusals that override everything above

  • No market cap means no auto-add, ever. The size buckets are gated on it, and for Bucket 1 — which has no floor — it stands in for “does this actually trade yet”. A Form 10 SpinCo has a core SIC code and no shares, so it cannot auto-add on the sector alone. A cap written as prose (~$3.7B) is refused too, and says so rather than claiming the cap is missing.
  • A ticker on the provenance removals list is never auto-added. A vendor has no idea a row was deliberately removed and will keep re-proposing it. An FMP biopharma screen re-proposed ALBT eight days after it was removed for leaving healthcare.
  • Auto means unasked, not unvalidated. Every auto-add routes through the same enrichment gate as a manual one, which refuses a half-filled row and says why.

What happens to your reply

A poller reads #ipo-spinoffs-newissues at 09:20, 13:20 and 18:20 ET. It reads both top-level channel messages and thread replies — where you post makes no difference. It only ever acts on a ticker already sitting pending in the ledger, it ignores everyone but you, and it is idempotent by message timestamp so a transient failure cannot re-fire an approval. It answers in the thread with what landed and what did not, then republishes exports/ so the change reaches the downstream consumers.

Company Summaries

Vylor Inc. (VYLR)

We breed and sell crop seed — mainly corn and soybeans under the Pioneer brand — to farmers worldwide, and license our genetics and trait technology to other seed companies. Farmers buy new seed every season, so revenue recurs annually and is priced per bag on the yield advantage our germplasm delivers. 2025 net sales were $9.9B with $2.6B of operating EBITDA; we were Corteva's seed segment until the October 2026 spin-off.

ADARx Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (ADRX)

We make siRNA drugs that silence the gene behind a disease so the harmful protein is never made. Our lead, onvuzosiran, shuts down liver production of plasma kallikrein to prevent hereditary angioedema attacks and is in Phase 3; behind it sit agazisiran for complement-driven kidney and eye disease and a Factor XI program for stroke prevention. We have no product revenue — AbbVie paid $335M upfront in 2025 to license our platform and owes milestones and royalties.

Accelevation Holdings Corp. (ACCV)

We design, build and install the power-distribution and structural systems that sit inside a data hall — remote power panels, busway and branch whips, steel framing and our SkyBridge modular overhead platform — and sell them to hyperscale, colocation and AI cloud operators. Revenue is project-based hardware plus installation, sold mostly to a handful of very large customers; 2025 sales were $448M, up 147%, with about $1.1B of backlog. Olympus Partners controls about 85% of the vote.

Vylor Inc. (VYLR) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Materials / Agricultural Inputs — seed and crop genetics Market Cap: ~$45.5B ($68.26, FMP, 2026-10-02) | Index: Newly public (NYSE, 2026-10-01); S&P 500 index change announced with the spin Closest comps: None on the sheet. Outside it: Bayer Crop Science (seed & traits), Syngenta (private), KWS Saat (KWS.DE), Limagrain (private) M&A FLAG: Spun off from Corteva (CTVA) on 2026-10-01 via 100% pro rata distribution, 1 VYLR per CTVA share. No acquisitions.

1. Business Description

We sell seed. Every spring a corn or soybean farmer in Iowa, Brazil or Ukraine buys a fresh bag — a hybrid's saved seed does not breed true, and patented traits cannot legally be replanted — so the purchase recurs every season. Our brands, led by Pioneer, are sold largely through our own agency network of farmer-sales reps in North America rather than through retail distributors, and we price each bag on the yield advantage our genetics deliver over the competitor's.

The business has two layers. The first is germplasm: the library of elite parent lines we have bred for a century and cross to produce each year's hybrids. That library is the moat — it cannot be recreated quickly, because each breeding cycle takes a growing season. The second is traits: insect resistance and herbicide tolerance stacked into those genetics, increasingly our own (Enlist, Vorceed, PowerCore) rather than licensed from Bayer, which used to collect a royalty on much of our acreage. Moving our acreage onto our own traits is the largest margin lever we have, and it is most of why segment EBITDA grew 19% in 2025 on 4% sales growth. We also license genetics and traits to independent seed companies — a royalty stream with almost no cost against it.

Scale: 2025 net sales of $9.90B (up 4%: +3% price, +2% volume, −1% FX) and operating EBITDA of $2.64B, a ~27% margin, up roughly 340bp. Corn is the large majority of revenue, soybeans second. North America is roughly half of sales; Latin America (Brazil's second-season safrinha corn crop, Argentina) is next and the most volatile, because it swings with Brazilian farm credit and the real.

What we are not: a chemicals company. Corteva kept crop protection, a more cyclical, more generic-exposed business. The market cap split — ~$45.5B for us against ~$8.4B left in Corteva — says which half investors were paying for.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - Replacing Bayer's traits with its own is a multi-year margin tailwind already visible: +340bp of EBITDA margin in 2025, with management pointing to a "$19B technology pipeline" of 12 platform launches across corn, soybean and wheat over the next decade. - Seed is a recurring, value-priced purchase in a four-player global oligopoly; standalone, it should trade as a pure-play genetics franchise rather than inside an ag-chem conglomerate. - Licensing to independent seed companies scales germplasm revenue at near-zero marginal cost.

Bear case: - Farm income: corn prices and US planted acreage drive volume and pricing; a weak corn year compresses both, and Brazil adds a credit-sensitive second leg. - ~$45.5B market cap plus ~$4B of debt against $2.64B of EBITDA is ~18x — a premium multiple that already assumes the trait-margin story is delivered. - Spin dynamics: Corteva holders who wanted the chemicals business, and funds that cannot hold the new name, are natural sellers over the first months.

Key swing factor: whether trait-royalty savings and pricing keep EBITDA growing double digits through a soft corn-price year.

ADARx Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (ADRX) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Biotech — RNA interference Market Cap: ~$2.0B ($19.54, FMP, 2026-10-02; $1.74B at the $17.00 offer) | Index: Newly public (Nasdaq Global Select, 2026-09-25) Closest comps: ALNY, ARWR, IONS, PHVS M&A FLAG: None (AbbVie collaboration, May 2025 — a license, not an acquisition)

1. Business Description

We make siRNA drugs: short double-stranded RNAs that ride into a cell, find the messenger RNA for one protein and destroy it, so the protein is never made. The category was proven by Alnylam and is now a mainstream modality; our claim is a better version of the chemistry (our "Messenger RNA Silencing Technology") and delivery beyond the liver ("Cell Targeted Delivery").

Our lead drug, onvuzosiran, silences plasma kallikrein in the liver to prevent attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE), the rare genetic disease in which patients suffer unpredictable, sometimes airway-threatening swelling. Early data showed up to a 93% reduction in plasma kallikrein, which in HAE is the variable that matters; our Phase 3 (STOP-HAE) reports topline data by the end of 2027. The pitch is dosing: an siRNA can suppress the target for months, so a patient could inject a few times a year rather than take a daily pill (Pharvaris, covered PHVS, and KalVista) or a frequent injectable. Behind it, agazisiran silences complement factor B — a "pipeline in a product" for complement-driven kidney disease, geographic atrophy and PNH, with Phase 2 data mid-to-late 2027 — and ADX-626 targets Factor XI for secondary stroke prevention. ADX-077 (obesity) and ADX-199 (Alzheimer's) are preclinical and carry the extrahepatic claim.

We have no product revenue. Our funding is AbbVie — which paid $335M upfront in May 2025 to license the platform for neurology, immunology and oncology targets, with up to $385M in option payments and up to $7.45B in milestones plus royalties — and equity: $427.3M of cash at 30 June 2026 before the IPO, plus $446.3M gross from 26.25M shares at $17.00 (upsized, top of range), and ~$89M from AbbVie in a concurrent placement that takes it to ~4.9%. Our co-founders, CEO Dr. Zhen Li and CTO Dr. Rui Zhu (formerly running siRNA programs at Arrowhead), built the platform.

Accelevation Holdings Corp. (ACCV) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Industrials / Electrical Equipment — data-center power distribution and white-space infrastructure Market Cap: ~$3.7B ($16.59 × 224.9M Class A + B shares, FMP, 2026-10-02) | Index: Newly public (Nasdaq Global Select, 2026-09-30) Closest comps: FPS (covered), GEV (covered); outside the sheet: VRT, ETN, NVT, POWL M&A FLAG: None disclosed in the last 12 months.

1. Business Description

We build the inside of a data hall. Once the shell, utility feed and switchgear exist, someone has to get power from the room's distribution down to every rack and hold up the cabling, busway and containment above them. We make the remote power panels (including 1,200-amp high-density units for AI racks), power distribution units and branch-circuit whips that carry the power, and the steel framing and SkyBridge modular overhead platform that carry everything else — and our crews install it. We sell it as "Design. Manufacture. Install.", which means a hyperscaler can hand us one scope instead of three.

Our customers are hyperscale cloud operators, colocation landlords and AI-cloud builders; substantially all revenue is data-center infrastructure. Revenue is project-based: we win a building or a campus, manufacture the content in our Ohio plants, and recognise it as it ships and installs. Average revenue per customer went from $0.7M in 2023 to $3.9M in 2025, and three hyperscalers now engage us at the full-platform design level — the point of the model, because a design-level relationship gets specified into every subsequent building.

Scale: 2025 net sales of $447.8M, up 147%; LTM to June 2026 ~$727M; backlog ~$1.1B with a 3.5x book-to-bill in Q2. Olympus Partners controls ~85% of the vote after the IPO, so this is a controlled company.

What is different, mechanically: prefabrication moves labour off a constrained job site into a factory, which matters when electricians, not capital, are the binding constraint on data-center construction. The company sizes its modular niche at $3.9B in 2025 growing ~35% a year — a company claim, not an audited figure.

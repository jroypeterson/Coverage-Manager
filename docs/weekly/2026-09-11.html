Three refusals that override everything above

  • No market cap means no auto-add, ever. The size buckets are gated on it, and for Bucket 1 — which has no floor — it stands in for “does this actually trade yet”. A Form 10 SpinCo has a core SIC code and no shares, so it cannot auto-add on the sector alone. A cap written as prose (~$3.7B) is refused too, and says so rather than claiming the cap is missing.
  • A ticker on the provenance removals list is never auto-added. A vendor has no idea a row was deliberately removed and will keep re-proposing it. An FMP biopharma screen re-proposed ALBT eight days after it was removed for leaving healthcare.
  • Auto means unasked, not unvalidated. Every auto-add routes through the same enrichment gate as a manual one, which refuses a half-filled row and says why.

What happens to your reply

A poller reads #ipo-spinoffs-newissues at 09:20, 13:20 and 18:20 ET. It reads both top-level channel messages and thread replies — where you post makes no difference. It only ever acts on a ticker already sitting pending in the ledger, it ignores everyone but you, and it is idempotent by message timestamp so a transient failure cannot re-fire an approval. It answers in the thread with what landed and what did not, then republishes exports/ so the change reaches the downstream consumers.

Two reply instructions last week were dead ends, and the auto-add reversal path is one of them

This is the finding I would act on, because it undercuts the justification for adding without asking.

scripts/poll_ipo_replies.py line 314 reads if ticker not in pending: ignored, and the set is built from candidate_ledger.pending(rows). Two consequences, both measured against the live ledger:

"Reply add EMAT and it goes in."

Ledger stateno row at all
What the poller doesignores it

"Reply decline 0625.HK and it comes out."

Ledger staterow exists, status approved
What the poller doesignores it

The second is the serious one. Buckets 1, 2 and 3 auto-add without asking, and the whole argument for that is that you can reverse it by replying. An auto-added name is written to the ledger as approved — which is exactly the status the poller refuses to act on. There is no reply that reverses an auto-add. Shein was auto-added at $26.4B, fell 21% below the bar within a day, and the report offered you a reversal the system could not perform.

data/ipo_reply_state.json shows last_scan: 2026-09-10T22:20Z and two historical applied timestamps, so the poller is running and has been running. It simply had nothing it was permitted to act on. Neither instruction has ever been exercised, which is why neither failure was visible.

Two changes, both in scripts/poll_ipo_replies.py, and both yours to approve:

1. decline should be accepted on an approved row, not only a pending one — reversing an auto-add is the case the instruction exists for. It should remove the row from the universe and flip the ledger to declined. 2. add on an unknown ticker should answer in-thread rather than being silently ignored. A reply that does nothing and says nothing is worse than an error.

I have changed no code. EMAT is queued this week as the narrow workaround for the sibling case.

RML — a vendor artifact that came within 14% of firing Bucket 2

Resolution Minerals listed ADSs on Nasdaq on 2026-09-09. FMP publishes $21.09B and yfinance $21.91B. The company's real market capitalisation is A$80.78M, about US$53M — a 414x overstatement, and I could not find a vendor that has it right.

The mechanism: each ADS represents 200 ordinary shares. Both vendors multiply the 2.57bn ordinary share count by the $8.53 ADS price. Nothing in the arithmetic looks impossible, which is the problem — Bucket 2 auto-adds any listing at $25B or above regardless of sector, and $21.9B is 14% short of writing a US$53M Idaho antimony explorer into the universe unasked. The only thing between the two was noticing that an ADS is not a share.

Worth knowing beyond this row: the ADS-ratio class will recur, every instance inflates rather than deflates, and the inflation lands in exactly the band the size-gated buckets read.

SOUNDHOUND AI, INC. (SOUN) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: SaaS / Conversational AI Market Cap: ~$2.79B | Index: Russell 2000, Nasdaq Closest comps: LPSN (acquired by SOUN), TWLO, NICE, VERI, U M&A FLAG: Not clean, and the deal is the reason this name is here. SoundHound completed its acquisition of LivePerson (LPSN) on 2026-09-04, issuing 37 million Class A shares and retiring LivePerson's debt at close. LivePerson common holders received roughly $42.8M of consideration in cash and stock; secured noteholders received roughly $261.2M. SoundHound also acquired Amelia (enterprise AI agents) and SYNQ3 (restaurant voice ordering) in 2024. Three acquisitions in under three years — this is a roll-up as much as an organic business, and the integration risk is real.

1. Business Description

We build the voice that answers when you call, drive up to, or type at another company. When you order at a Five Guys drive-thru, ask your Stellantis or Hyundai car for directions, or open a chat window with a large bank, there is a reasonable chance the thing responding is ours, running under our customer's brand rather than ours. We are invisible on purpose: we sell infrastructure, not a consumer product.

We reach customers three ways, and they have quite different economics. Restaurants are our most visible business — drive-thru and phone-order agents at chains including Five Guys, IHOP and Jersey Mike's, priced per location and per interaction, which makes revenue scale with store count and order volume rather than with headcount saved. Automotive is the oldest and stickiest — our voice assistant is embedded in vehicles from Stellantis, Hyundai/Kia and several Chinese OEMs, and we earn a per-unit royalty at build plus a recurring connected-services fee for the life of the vehicle, so a design win in 2024 is still paying us in 2032. Enterprise is where we are now spending, and where LivePerson lands: agentic customer-service deployments sold as multi-year subscriptions with usage-based overage, including an eight-figure multi-year contract across more than 20 Latin American countries and a seven-figure agreement with a Chinese automotive infotainment software company, both signed in the second quarter.

What LivePerson adds is the channel we did not have. We were voice; LivePerson is enterprise digital messaging — the chat, SMS and in-app layer inside large contact centres, with a long-standing base of financial-services and telecom customers. The pitch to a customer is now one vendor across voice and text rather than two, which matters because contact-centre buyers hate integrating two AI stacks against one CRM.

The differentiator we would actually defend is the speech-to-meaning architecture: rather than transcribing audio to text and then parsing the text, our stack resolves audio directly to intent. In a drive-thru that is the difference between a usable system and a frustrating one, because latency and background noise are what kill voice ordering, not vocabulary. Whether that edge survives a year of frontier-model improvement is the open question, and it is the bear case below.

Scale, so the enthusiasm is anchored: Q2 2026 revenue was $61.9M, up 45% year over year and 40% sequentially, at a 58% non-GAAP gross margin. Full-year 2026 guidance is $230-260M, set before the LivePerson contribution, against $168.9M in 2025 and $84.7M in 2024. We are not profitable: the Q2 GAAP net loss was $42.8M, the non-GAAP loss $9M, and adjusted EBITDA loss improved 33% year over year. We hold $203M of cash against $5.4M of debt.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case - Revenue compounding at 45%+ with a guided $230-260M year, from $84.7M two years earlier, and the LivePerson base has not yet been added to guidance — the number that goes into the next update is structurally higher than the one in the market. - Automotive royalties are genuinely annuity-like: a design win pays per unit at build and then per year through the connected-services contract, so the installed base is a growing floor under revenue that does not require re-selling. - The Oasis platform is shortening sales cycles measurably — management cites an eight-figure commitment signed within 90 days of first demo, which for enterprise contact-centre software is unusual and is the specific claim to test next quarter.

Bear case - 38.5% of the float is short — 156.9M shares against a 401M float, a 5.15-day cover ratio. EVOLUTION METALS & TECHNOLOGIES CORP. (EMAT) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Materials / Rare Earth & Critical Materials Market Cap: ~$1.83B (FMP) / ~$1.92B (yfinance) | Index: Russell 2000 and Russell 3000 preliminary IPO addition, effective 2026-09-21 Closest comps: MP, SSMR, LIN M&A FLAG: None in the last 12 months. The company became public via a 2026 business combination and has made no material acquisitions or divestitures since.

1. Business Description

We are trying to build a rare-earth permanent-magnet supply chain that does not pass through China, and we are being paid to do it by a regulatory deadline rather than by a market preference. From 1 January 2027, the US DFARS mine-to-magnet restriction bars Chinese-origin rare-earth magnets from US defence hardware. China currently controls roughly 90% of global sintered NdFeB magnet capacity. That gap is the whole business.

Our operating asset is a plant in Pohang, South Korea, which we are expanding toward approximately 10,000 metric tons a year of rare-earth magnet capacity, of which about 6,000 tons is high-performance sintered NdFeB — the grade that goes into traction motors, actuators, guidance systems and wind turbines. We have secured Tier-1 OEM quality certifications across six magnet grades, which is the gate that separates a plant from a supplier: an automotive or defence customer will not qualify a magnet source without it, and qualification takes quarters, not weeks. We also completed a first non-China rare-earth feedstock shipment ahead of the DFARS date.

We sell magnets and processed critical materials to defence primes, automotive OEMs and industrial manufacturers under offtake and supply agreements. The revenue model is ordinary industrial manufacturing — tonnage times price, with margin determined by feedstock cost and yield. There is no subscription, no take rate and no recurring software revenue anywhere in this business, and anyone describing it as a technology company is describing the ticker rather than the P&L.

We are pre-scale, and the numbers say so unambiguously. Q1 2026 revenue was $1.9M, Q2 2026 was $1.6M, and full-year fiscal 2026 is guided to $5-8M. Fiscal 2027 is guided to $400-460M. That is a roughly sixty-fold step-up in one year, and essentially all of it is capacity that has not been built, qualified or sold yet.

Three refusals that override everything above

UNITREE ROBOTICS TECHNOLOGY CO., LTD. (688836.SS) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Tech / Industrial Robotics & Automation (legged robots and drive components) Market Cap: ~$40.5B (RMB 272B) | Index: Shanghai STAR Market, listed 2026-08-19; not yet index-included Closest comps: 2715.HK (Estun), TSLA (Optimus), ISRG (robotics, different application), 688825.SS (CXMT — same exchange, not a peer business) M&A FLAG: None. No acquisition, divestiture or liquidation in the last 12 months. The only capital event is the IPO itself: RMB 6.1B (~$905M) raised on 2026-08-06, listed 2026-08-19.

1. Business Description

We sell robots with legs, and we sell the motors that make legs work.

Two product families sit on one drivetrain. The quadrupeds — Go2, B2, A2 — are four-legged machines that walk into places wheels cannot: substations, tunnels, construction sites, university labs, and a surprising amount of film and event work. The humanoids — G1, H1, R1, H2 — are bipeds, and they are the reason anyone knows our name, because they are the ones doing backflips and side-kicks in videos with tens of millions of views. Underneath both is the same vertically-integrated stack: we design and manufacture our own harmonic reducers, joint actuators, motors, sensors and controllers rather than buying them from Harmonic Drive or Nabtesco. That is not a marketing point, it is the margin. Gross margin ran 44.75% in FY2023, 57.22% in FY2024 and 60.44% in FY2025 — a 16-point expansion over two years, on a hardware business, during a period when average selling prices were falling. A humanoid that cost far more two years ago now sells for roughly RMB 166,400 (~$25,000). Cutting price and lifting margin at the same time is what owning the actuator supply chain buys.

The scale is real but early. FY2025 revenue was RMB 1.70B (~$250M), up 335% year on year, against RMB 392.8M in FY2024 and RMB 159.1M in FY2023. We recognised 5,215 humanoid units and shipped 5,511; we recognised 23,037 quadrupeds and produced 26,032. Humanoids passed quadrupeds in revenue for the first time in FY2025 — RMB 867.8M (51.8% of main business) against RMB 697.6M (41.6%) — having been RMB 3.0M, or 1.9% of revenue, as recently as FY2023. Components sold to third parties are RMB 103.7M (6.2%). No customer is more than half of revenue and the top five are 12.08% of the total, which is unusually diffuse for hardware at this scale and tells you what the buyer actually is: not one industrial anchor customer, but several thousand research groups, integrators and corporates buying one to ten units each.

We are also, and this is the genuinely differentiating fact, profitable. Attributable net profit was RMB 278.2M in FY2025 on a 16.4% net margin, against RMB 95.5M in FY2024 and a RMB 11.1M loss in FY2023. Operating profit was RMB 367.7M. R&D was RMB 145M — 8.5% of revenue, which is low for the category and is the number a bear should attack first. For comparison, UBTech has accumulated more than RMB 5.6B of losses over six years pursuing the same market, and the American pure-plays (Figure, Agility, 1X) have no meaningful revenue at all. We guided H1 2026 revenue to RMB 1.052–1.128B, which annualises above FY2025 without assuming a second-half acceleration.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case

  • It is the only profitable pure-play, and profitability at this stage is a structural fact, not a quarter. RMB 278M of attributable net profit on RMB 1.70B of revenue, with gross margin up 16 points in two years while ASPs fell, is evidence that the vertical-integration strategy works. UBTech has lost RMB 5.6B over six years chasing the same buyer; Figure, Agility and 1X have essentially no revenue. If humanoids become a real category, Unitree enters it earning money while its competitors are still funding losses.
  • Duopoly share of the domestic market. Unitree and AgiBot together are projected to take roughly 80% of Chinese humanoid shipments in 2026. Counterpoint counts more than 100 Chinese humanoid companies today and expects consolidation to "a few dozen" within 12–24 months — public-market access is what decides which side of that consolidation a company lands on, and Unitree now has it plus RMB 6.1B of fresh capital.
  • The component business is an option nobody is paying for. Harmonic reducers and joint actuators are RMB 103.7M today, but they are the scarcest input in the whole category. Selling them to the ninety-odd Chinese humanoid startups that cannot make their own is a picks-and-shovels business that grows whoever wins the robot war.

Bear case

  • The valuation is the entire bear case and it is sufficient on its own. ~978x trailing earnings, ~24x FY2025 revenue, and a market cap that went from $9.1B to $50.9B in one trading session on a book that was oversubscribed 8,000x by retail. This is A-share IPO mechanics — tiny float, capped offer price, retail scramble — not a considered institutional mark. Recent Chinese robotics and embodied-AI listings show wide post-listing dispersion, and several have taken steep drawdowns.
  • The demand may not be what it looks like. The buyer base is research labs, universities, integrators and entertainment — not factories running these things in production at scale. Analysts have been explicit: "in the absence of a significant improvement in the AI model capability of robot makers, the current humanoid robot shipment upcycle is unlikely to be sustained over the next 1-2 years." 5,215 humanoid units is a demo-and-pilot number, and demo-and-pilot demand can stop.
  • AgiBot is bigger on forward revenue and is coming to the public market too. It is chasing a Hong Kong listing on US$593M of expected 2026 revenue — above anything Unitree has guided to. The "only profitable pure-play" argument weakens considerably if the larger competitor lists and turns out to be profitable as well.
  • R&D is 8.5% of revenue in a category whose whole thesis is that the AI gets better. That is a low number for a company priced as an AI winner.

Key swing factor

Whether humanoid unit demand in 2026–27 comes from production deployments or stays research, pilot and entertainment — because at 8.5% R&D intensity and a shared drivetrain, Unitree is priced as an AI platform while it is structurally a very good hardware manufacturer, and only production demand closes that gap.

4. What to Watch

  • H1 2026 results (Aug–Sep 2026). Guided RMB 1.052–1.128B. Two things matter more than the top line: whether the EBIT margin stops falling, and whether humanoid ASPs hold near RMB 166k or keep sliding.
  • The AgiBot Hong Kong listing (timing not set). A direct read on whether the "only profitable one" argument survives, and the first competitive comp for the multiple.
  • STAR 50 / index inclusion and the first lock-up expiries. STAR listings have a very small initial free float; the pre-IPO holder unlocks are the first real supply test and the first honest price discovery.
  • Chinese humanoid consolidation, next 12–24 months. Counterpoint's "100 to a few dozen" call. Watch for failures and distressed acquisitions among the sub-scale ninety — each one is both a share gain and a component-sales loss.

5. Investor Positioning & Sentiment

Positioning is the least informative thing about this name right now, and that is itself the finding. The IPO was oversubscribed roughly 8,000x by retail investors, and the debut ran +629% intraday before closing +460% and then giving back roughly 20% over the following session. That is a retail-dominated, tiny-float A-share tape, not an institutional book.

There is no meaningful sell-side rating distribution yet — the stock is two days old and STAR coverage typically starts after the first reported period. Short interest is not a usable signal: A-share shorting requires securities-lending eligibility that a fresh STAR listing does not have. The relevant flow story is passive: Unitree is a candidate for STAR Market and China-robotics index inclusion, and it is directly relevant to KSTR (KraneShares STAR Market) and KOID (KraneShares Humanoid Robotics) — forced buying that arrives on a schedule, into a float that is too small to absorb it comfortably.

For our purposes the sentiment read is simple: this is the first public-market price on a pure-play humanoid, which makes it the benchmark every private humanoid mark and every robotics capex forecast will now be argued against — whether or not the price itself is right.

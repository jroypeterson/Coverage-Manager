VOGENX, INC. (VOGX) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Clinical-stage metabolic disease Market Cap: ~$196M | Index: None (IPO 2026-08-12; Russell-eligible at the September window) Closest comps: NVO, LLY, VKTX, CRNX M&A FLAG: None. No acquisitions, divestitures or liquidations in the past 12 months. The company IPO'd on 12 August 2026 and has done nothing else.

1. Business Description

We sell nothing yet. What we have is a single molecule with an unusually clean mechanical story and a patient population that currently has no approved treatment at all.

Mizagliflozin is an oral, minimally absorbed selective inhibitor of SGLT1. The distinction matters, and it is the whole company. SGLT2 inhibitors — the Jardiance and Farxiga class — work in the kidney and act systemically. SGLT1 is the transporter that pulls glucose out of the small intestine, and because our molecule barely enters the bloodstream, it acts where the food is and essentially nowhere else. We are not lowering blood sugar by flushing it out; we are slowing how fast it arrives in the first place.

The lead indication follows directly from that. Post-bariatric hypoglycemia is what happens to a subset of gastric-bypass patients when the anatomy that used to meter glucose delivery is no longer there: a meal dumps sugar into the gut, the pancreas over-responds with insulin, and blood glucose crashes an hour or two later. Patients experience confusion, syncope, and in severe cases seizures. There is no FDA-approved therapy; the standard of care is dietary management, which works poorly. We are attacking the mechanism at its origin — flatten the absorption curve and the insulin overshoot does not happen.

We have administered mizagliflozin to more than 500 subjects across 10 clinical studies, and in Phase 2 (study VGX 001-012) the 5 mg dose reduced Level 3 hypoglycemia events by 0.27 per day versus placebo (p=0.023). A second Phase 2 also read out positive. Note the pedigree: this is not a novel chemical entity we discovered — it is a re-purposed asset with a large existing safety database, which is why a company that has raised only ~$11.5 million since its 2021 founding is sitting on ten completed studies.

Two more indications extend the same mechanism. Gastroparesis (Phase 2 planned 2027) is the mirror image — delayed gastric emptying — and is, not incidentally, the best-characterised side effect of GLP-1 therapy. GIP-dependent Cushing's syndrome is a rare endocrine indication where food-stimulated GIP release drives cortisol production. One molecule, three shots on goal, all downstream of how glucose moves through the gut.

How we will make money: we do not, yet. If approved, this is a specialty-pharma pricing model — a small, identifiable, severely symptomatic population reached through bariatric surgery centres and endocrinologists, with high per-patient pricing and modest commercial infrastructure.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - An approved-therapy vacuum. Post-bariatric hypoglycemia has no FDA-approved treatment. First approval takes the category, and the mechanism is causal rather than palliative. - Safety risk is unusually low for a Phase 2 name. 500+ subjects dosed across 10 studies, and minimal systemic absorption structurally limits off-target exposure. Most binary biotech risk is safety risk; a good deal of it is already retired here. - Optionality is levered to a franchise that is still growing. Gastroparesis is the canonical GLP-1 side effect. As NVO and LLY expand the treated population, the addressable pool for the second indication expands with it, at no cost to Vogenx.

Bear case: - Single asset, single mechanism. Three indications do not diversify a molecule. One safety signal or one failed Phase 2b and the equity is close to cash. - The population may be smaller than the story. Post-bariatric hypoglycemia is a subset of a subset — severe, diagnosed cases are far fewer than the bariatric-surgery denominator implies, and payers will interrogate the diagnostic criteria hard. - Financed to 2028 and no further. Runway covers a Phase 2b readout, not a Phase 3, not an NDA and not a launch. On a 13.9M-share base, the next raise is heavily dilutive unless data re-rates the stock first.

Key swing factor: whether the Phase 2b in post-bariatric hypoglycemia converts a pharmacodynamic endpoint — fewer measured glucose excursions — into a symptomatic endpoint the FDA will register on. The mechanism is not in doubt; the regulatory endpoint is.

BIOSTEM TECHNOLOGIES, INC. (BSEM) — Quick Background

Sector / Industry: Biopharma / Regenerative wound care (placental allografts) Market Cap: ~$55M | Index: None (Nasdaq Capital Market since 2026-08-07; OTC before that) Closest comps: MDXG, ORGO, IART M&A FLAG: No mergers, acquisitions or divestitures in the past 12 months. But this row carries an ACCOUNTING FLAG that matters more than an M&A flag would: FY2024 net revenue was restated from a preliminary $301.8M to $69.7M (filed November 2025), the external auditor was changed to KPMG, and FY2024/FY2025 audits were completed only in Q1 2026 as a precondition of the Nasdaq uplisting.

1. Business Description

We take donated human placental tissue — material that is otherwise discarded after live birth — and process it into thin, sheet-form allografts that a clinician lays directly onto a chronic wound. Our processing method, BioREtain, is the differentiator we sell on: conventional processing devitalises the tissue, and ours is designed to preserve the native structural proteins and growth factors that make the graft biologically active rather than merely a covering. The products are Vendaje, Vendaje AC and Vendaje Optic.

Our customers are wound-care clinics, hospital outpatient departments and physician offices treating diabetic foot ulcers and venous leg ulcers — wounds that will not close on their own and that, untreated, lead to amputation. Clinically we are selling avoided amputations and faster closure. Commercially we are selling a reimbursable product code.

And that is the business, honestly stated: we are a Medicare reimbursement business wearing a biologics coat. Revenue is collected almost entirely through product-specific Q-codes under Medicare, at prices CMS sets. Volume matters, but the price per graft is an administrative decision made in Baltimore, not a negotiation with a customer. Every material inflection in our financial history — up and down — traces to a CMS policy change or to how we account for the distributor that reaches those clinicians.

Scale is now modest and shrinking. Q2 2026 net revenue was $7.9 million, up 29% sequentially from $6.1 million but down 28% from $11.0 million in Q2 2025. Gross margin was 61% ($4.8M gross profit). We guide to $26–29 million for FY2026 against $47.5 million in FY2025.

The restatement, plainly. We previously reported FY2024 net revenue of $301.8 million. We now report $69.7 million for the same year. Nothing about the underlying commercial activity changed: the fees paid to our distributor, Venture Medical, were reclassified from sales-and-marketing expense to contra-revenue under US GAAP, which reduced reported revenue and reported expense by the same amount. It was not a fraud finding. It does mean that anyone who valued this company on its 2024 headline was valuing a number four times larger than the one that survived audit.

3. Investment Debate

Bull case: - 61% gross margin on a reimbursed biologic, with sequential revenue growth of 29% in Q2 — the stabilisation, if it holds, comes off a genuinely low base. - The exchange listing is itself the catalyst. Completing KPMG audits of two restated years and clearing Nasdaq's listing standards is a real credibility gate that most restatement stories never pass. It also unlocks institutional buyers and financing routes closed to an OTC issuer. - Sector read-through value. Whatever happens to the equity, BSEM is a high-frequency instrument for reading CMS skin-substitute policy, which drives MDXG and ORGO — both covered.

Bear case: - Revenue is in a two-year decline: $69.7M → $47.5M → $26–29M guided. Guidance implies the business roughly halves again this year. Sequential growth off a Q1 trough does not offset a 28% year-on-year fall. - $7.0M of cash against $5.5M of quarterly burn, with no raise attached to the uplisting. Dilution at a $55M market cap is the base case, and the company has limited leverage over timing. - CMS is the whole risk and it is unstable. The CY2026 skin-substitute final rule was issued, then BSEM's products were placed on a 12-month status-quo list, then all seven LCDs were withdrawn on 7 January 2026. Pricing for the entire category is unsettled, and a business that earns 100% of its revenue at administered prices has no hedge against that. - Restatement history caps the multiple. A company that reported $301.8M and delivered $69.7M for the same year will be discounted by institutions for several clean quarters, deservedly.

Key swing factor: how CMS finally reprices skin substitutes after withdrawing the LCDs — that single decision sets the revenue base for BSEM, MDXG and ORGO simultaneously, and BSEM is the one of the three with no balance sheet to absorb a bad outcome.
